About Me

Hi! I’m Cen-Jhih, and you can also call me Steven. I’m a Ph.D. student at the Kahlert School of Computing, University of Utah. I’m fortunate to be advised by Prof. Aditya Bhaskara. Before my Ph.D., I was a research assistant at the Institute of Statistical Science, Academia Sinica, where I worked with Prof. Su-Yun Huang on robust aggregation for federated learning. I also worked as a data scientist at Data-on-Demand, applying machine learning to business decisions for clients. I received my M.S. in Statistics from National Chengchi University, where I worked on missing-not-at-random data with Prof. Kwei Tang and Prof. Tsung-Chi Cheng, and my B.S. in Applied Mathematics from National Chiao Tung University.

I’m broadly interested in utilizing algorithmic and mathematical/statistical approaches to solve real-world problems, especially making large language models more efficient to adapt and serve without sacrificing quality. Currently, I’m working on KV-cache selection for sparse attention in long-context inference and on KV-cache quantization, including both algorithm design and GPU kernel optimization using tools such as Triton to achieve real speedups and make these methods practical. Previously, I worked on structured sparse fine-tuning for quantized LLMs (TMLR 2025) and studied how LLMs utilize context in in-context learning. I also enjoy following the open-source training and inference ecosystem, including PyTorch, vLLM, and llama.cpp, and I’m interested in bringing my methods into these frameworks.

Research

Efficient LLM Inference

KV-cache compression, including sparse attention, quantization, and KV-cache eviction, to reduce memory traffic and latency during decoding. I am also interested in practical kernel design to build systems that achieve real inference speedups.

Memory-Efficient Adaptation

Structured sparse fine-tuning that selects important neurons and updates them with regular dense operations, using memory-efficient zeroth-order importance estimation and low-quantization-error initialization for quantized LLMs.

Context Utilization

How LLMs utilize context: studying how to retrieve better demonstrations for in-context learning and long-context reasoning, and understanding how models utilize that context, such as how the position, quality, content itself, and ordering of the context affect the final generation.

AI4Science

Studying how to apply neural networks to solve PDEs and applying them to real-world physical simulation problems.

Published

  1. An Efficient Sparse Fine-Tuning with Low Quantization Error via Neural Network Pruning
    Cen-Jhih Li, Aditya Bhaskara
    TMLR 2025 Transactions on Machine Learning Research
    arXiv OpenReview Code Post: Overview Post: Zeroth-Order
    Fine-tuning is an important step in adapting foundation models such as large language models to downstream tasks. To make this step more accessible to users with limited computational budgets, it is crucial to develop fine-tuning methods that are memory and computationally efficient. Sparse Fine-tuning (SpFT) and Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) are two frameworks that have emerged for addressing this problem and have been adopted widely in practice. In this work, we develop a new SpFT framework, based on ideas from neural network pruning. At a high level, we first identify "important" neurons/nodes using feature importance metrics from network pruning (specifically, we use the structural pruning method), and then perform fine-tuning by restricting to weights involving these neurons. Experiments on common language tasks show our method improves SpFT's memory efficiency by 20–50% while matching the accuracy of state-of-the-art methods like LoRA's variants.

  2. Robust Aggregation for Federated Learning by Minimum γ-Divergence Estimation
    Cen-Jhih Li, P.-H. Huang, Y.-T. Ma, H. Hung, Su-Yun Huang
    Entropy 2022 Entropy, 24(5), 686
    Paper Code Post
    Federated learning is a framework for multiple devices or institutions, called local clients, to collaboratively train a global model without sharing their data. For federated learning with a central server, an aggregation algorithm integrates model information sent from local clients to update the parameters for a global model. Sample mean is the simplest and most commonly used aggregation method. However, it is not robust for data with outliers or under the Byzantine problem, where Byzantine clients send malicious messages to interfere with the learning process. Some robust aggregation methods were introduced in literature including marginal median, geometric median and trimmed-mean. In this article, we propose an alternative robust aggregation method, named γ-mean, which is the minimum divergence estimation based on a robust density power divergence. This γ-mean aggregation mitigates the influence of Byzantine clients by assigning fewer weights. This weighting scheme is data-driven and controlled by the γ value. Robustness from the viewpoint of the influence function is discussed and some numerical results are presented.
